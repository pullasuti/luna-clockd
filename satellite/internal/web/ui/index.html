Alarms

checking…
New alarm
The radio plays the station at the alarm time. Without the stick (or with it in use for a station search) the alarm rings with the timer sound.
The song plays from the USB drive and repeats until you stop the alarm. If the drive or the song isn't there, or the song doesn't start within 3 seconds, the alarm rings with the timer sound.
While it rings: the alarm button or "stop" stops it, any other button snoozes it. ?
The sound starts quieter and reaches full volume in 30 seconds, and rings for up to 5 minutes. Alarms work without Home Assistant, but the clock has no battery-backed clock: after a power cut nothing rings until it has the time again. After a sunrise alarm the light stays on: a short press of the alarm button turns it off (or it goes off by itself after 30 minutes). Snoozing shows the minutes on the face; pressing a button again while they show makes it 5 minutes longer (10, 15, 20, then 5 again). The skip button next to a repeating alarm leaves out its next ring only.
On the clock
A short press of the alarm button shows the next alarm; volume up and down step through the others. Hold the alarm button for 2 seconds to set the alarm shown, or a new one: volume up and down change the blinking part, a short alarm press goes on to the next part (repeat, hours, minutes, AM/PM) and saves after the last. To delete the alarm shown, press the alarm button and volume down together briefly. The action button skips the next ring of the alarm shown ("skip"; again: "on"), or switches a one-time alarm on and off.

Timers

Gentle is the easiest to stop by voice; the action button always works. ?
Gentle: beep-beep, long pause. Chime: ding-dong, long pause. Classic: beep-beep every second. Urgent: fast high beeps. The tunes play a few seconds of melody and then pause. "Stop" is heard in the pauses, so sounds with short pauses are harder to stop by voice.
Timers are set by voice through Home Assistant (“set a timer for 10 minutes”).

FM radio

checking…
Stations and playing: the Player card. Here: the stick, the stream link and the tuner settings.
Stream link
For Home Assistant, Music Assistant or any player on your network. ?
The link plays what the clock's radio plays, as a FLAC stream (lossless at 32 kHz, which holds all of FM's sound; about 0.8 Mbit/s, so for your own network). A player that can't take FLAC gets WAV (1.5 Mbit/s) with .wav in place of .flac. When the clock plays nothing, the link starts a station of its own without the clock's speaker: add ?mhz=91.2 to pick it, otherwise it takes the last station. One stick tunes one station, so a link can't change the station the clock is playing. Stop ends the radio for everyone. Anyone with the link can listen: makes a new one and cuts off the old.
Region
Reception
Weak gives the tuner more gain; Strong less, for when stations bleed into each other. Both apply from the next station played, and a new search finds stations with them.
Supported sticks
RTL2832U + R820T / R820T2Tested: a generic RTL2838UHIDIR stick (USB 0bda:2838)
The radio is experimental. Other receivers (RTL-SDR Blog V4 / R828D, E4000, FC0013 and others) are not supported yet: the clock then says the stick's tuner is not supported. ?
The clock drives the stick itself, without vendor drivers, so each receiver chip needs its own small piece of code. The radio is being split up so that support for a new receiver can come as a self-contained addition: if you have another stick and would like to help, open an issue or a pull request on GitHub.
Needs a supported stick in the USB socket, with System › USB on USB drive (on an unlocked clock a restart switches the socket back to adb). ?
The clock tunes the stick itself, no internet or Home Assistant needed. The region sets the band (Japan: 76-95 MHz) and the de-emphasis (75 µs in the Americas, 50 µs elsewhere). Find stations searches the band in about half a minute and lists the stations that come in in stereo. A station's name (RDS) shows once it has played for a few seconds. Stereo fades to mono when the signal is weak, which keeps it free of hiss. Volume, the stop word and the sleep timer work as for any music. Home Assistant can start a station too: play media with the id fm:91.2. This device in the Player plays the radio in this browser.

Wake word

Up to two at a time. Home Assistant can also switch them from the device page. More wake words…
Slightly gives the fewest false wakes; very works best with a TV or music on.
Used while the clock plays sound. ?
The clock's own sound partly drowns your voice; one step more sensitive while it plays helps. When music plays and the clock thinks it may have heard the word, it also turns the music down for about 4 seconds: if it didn't answer, just say it again. Attempts made then are marked ♪ below.
The clocks that heard the wake word compare how clearly they heard it (your voice over the room's noise) and only the best one answers; the others stay quiet. It takes a fifth of a second and works between clocks on the same network. Switched off, Home Assistant still lets only one clock answer: the one whose request reached it first.

More wake words

About 900 community phrases. This browser fetches them from GitHub; the clock stays offline. ?
From the ESPHome collection (its "experiment" ones are lightly trained) and Tater Totterson's community collection (many of them silly). This browser downloads the list and the model from github.com and hands the model to the clock. Quality varies a lot: some want a very particular pronunciation. Tater's newer models (V6 and up) are bigger: each one switched on uses about 2.5 times the processor time of an ESPHome word (roughly a fifth of one core instead of a thirteenth). Add one, switch it on under Wake word, and watch Recent attempts.

USB

checking…
Its music: Audio › Player.
One socket: a USB drive and adb over a cable exclude each other. A restart always returns to adb. ?
Switching to a USB drive moves the socket to host mode and mounts the drive read-only (FAT and ext2-4). Nothing on the clock runs anything from the drive. A drive can be plugged in or pulled out at any time while the socket is on USB drive. Switching back to adb is possible once nothing is plugged in. Switching takes a few seconds either way. exFAT and NTFS drives are not supported.

Keys

A root shell on port 22 for keys listed here (no passwords). Paste a public key: the line from ~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub. ?
Log in with ssh root@clock. Anyone with a listed key gets full control of the clock: add only your own. Switching SSH off keeps the keys; shells already open stay until they end. Without keys SSH doesn't run.

Update

checking…
Off (the default), the clock never contacts the internet. On, it asks GitHub's API once a day (and when you press Check now) for the latest release of the project; only the version number is read. Download fetches that release's package from GitHub; the clock then checks it like an uploaded one (signature, every file) and shows what would change. Installing is always your click. The clock's time must be known (it comes from Home Assistant).
Off (the default), Home Assistant always sees the clock as up to date. On, a newer release found by the check shows up on the clock's Firmware entity in Home Assistant, with its release note; Install there downloads it and installs it the same way as here (signature, every file, automatic rollback), and the clock restarts. Home Assistant's voice assistant setup installs any update it sees, so leave this off while adding the clock to Home Assistant.
What's new in this version

      A release package (clockd-version-arm64.tar.gz). Only signed packages are accepted. ?
      The clock checks the whole package before changing anything: the signature, every file, and that the new clockd starts. Settings, keys and wake word models stay as they are. The old version is kept: if the new one doesn't keep running well through its first minute (it stops, hangs or its page doesn't answer), the clock goes back to it by itself within five minutes. Afterwards you can still go back to it here, until the next update. Voice and display pause for a few seconds during the restart.

      Backup

      All settings in one file: clock face, lights, sound, voice, night schedule, Music Assistant, time. Not included: Wi-Fi passwords, the Home Assistant token, the ESPHome key, logged-in browsers, installed wake word models.
